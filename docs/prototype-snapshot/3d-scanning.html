Accurate scans, not just pretty ones

Plenty of scanners produce something that looks right. We measure. Our system holds dimensional accuracy to 0.02 mm in every axis, which is the difference between a picture of your part and a file you can build from.

The newest addition to the workshop is a self tracking scanner, so there are no stickers, no markers, and no chalky spray on the object we are capturing. That matters when the thing in your hands is old, delicate, or the only one left.

Rates are competitive, and the deliverables are ones you can hand to an engineer with confidence.

The hard ones

Some jobs take a steady hand

Scanning a rare piece is rarely one and done. A turn of the century street lamp we digitized took multiple detailed sessions, a steady hand, and a fair amount of determination before the data was clean enough to build from.

That is the part most people do not see, and it is exactly why the file you get back is worth having. Complex geometry, deep recesses, and reflective surfaces all get handled rather than glossed over.

Common questions

Good to know

Do you need to put stickers or spray on my part?
No. Our self tracking scanner works without markers and without scanning spray, so nothing is applied to your object. That is what makes it safe for antiques and finished surfaces.
How accurate is it really?
Dimensional accuracy of 0.02 mm in X, Y, and Z. For most mechanical work that is far tighter than the tolerance the part actually needs, which is the point.
What can you scan?
Objects from small to large. Very small, highly detailed items and large assemblies both get handled, though the approach changes. Tell us what it is and we will tell you honestly if it suits scanning.
Can I get the file, or do you keep it?
You can have the file. Standard formats are available on request so you are never locked in.
What does a scan cost?
It depends on size, complexity, and how clean the result has to be, so we quote per job. Rates are competitive and there are no surprises after the fact.
